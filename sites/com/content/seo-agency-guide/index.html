SEO agency guide: how to choose in 2026

TL;DR – SEO changed (GEO, AI Overviews, AI citations). To choose an SEO agency, ask sharper questions, reject “#1 guaranteed” pitches, and demand a measurable method tied to inquiries — not rankings alone.

Looking for an SEO agency and not sure where to start? Be picky. Search changed a lot since 2024. “#1 in 90 days” promises rarely survive contact with Google — or with ChatGPT.

This guide gives you practical criteria for choosing an SEO agency in 2026: what changed, what to ask, and which traps to skip.

SEO in 2026 is not SEO in 2023

Three shifts matter for small businesses:

  • GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) — ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini answer before people click. An agency that ignores AI engines is stuck in 2023. See our ChatGPT SEO / GEO page.
  • Google AI Overviews — AI answers sit above classic results. More clicks go to sources AI systems trust and cite.
  • Brand and entity consistency — backlinks still help, but AI citations, clear entities and multi-channel proof weigh more than before.

Five questions to ask any SEO agency

1. “How do you handle GEO?”

If they blank, walk away. Strong teams talk FAQ structure, Schema, citable facts and named entities — not just “keywords.”

2. “Which KPIs do you track?”

Beware agencies that only sell rankings. Ask for Search Console impressions, CTR, qualified traffic, calls/forms, AI citations and Google reviews.

3. “Which case studies match my market?”

Ask for live URLs. Check the site, Maps presence and reviews yourself. Our case studies show SMB outcomes.

4. “Who writes the content?”

AI can draft. Humans still need to edit for usefulness and business intent — or “helpful content” updates will punish you.

5. “How do you build links?”

PBNs and bought links are a liability. Prefer useful content, editorial partnerships and clean local citations (NAP).

Local, national or GEO-specialist — which fit?

In one sentence

For a nearby SMB, an agency that owns local SEO, Google Maps and GEO beats a distant national factory process.

  • Local-focused — best for Maps, reviews and neighborhood demand. See also small business SEO.
  • National / enterprise — broader teams, heavier process, higher retainers.
  • GEO / AI specialist — useful when buyers already ask ChatGPT before Google.
  • Freelance consultant — flexible for smaller scopes; still demand a clear method.

Traps to avoid

  • “We guarantee #1 on Google” — nobody can honestly guarantee a specific rank.
  • 80-page audits with no execution — PDFs are not a monthly system.
  • Opaque subcontracting — you should know who touches your site and GBP.
  • SEO without conversion — traffic without inquiries is a cost. Click First™ connects visibility to demand.

Classic SEO vs GEO

Classic SEO targets Google (and Maps) results. GEO targets AI-generated answers. Shared foundations: clear content, Schema.org, consistent entities, reviews. In 2026 a serious agency does both.

How to judge results after six months

  • Qualified inquiries (calls, forms, bookings) — the business KPI.
  • Local visibility (Maps, reviews, brand + city queries).
  • Money pages moving — not only satellite blog posts.
  • AI citations on your key prompts.
  • Transparent reporting: work done + next-month priorities.
